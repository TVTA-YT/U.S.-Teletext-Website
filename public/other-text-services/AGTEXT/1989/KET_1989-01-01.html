     LEAD IN DRINKING WATER

      
      

      
      


      
      

      
      
Farm families who use private

      
      

      
      
water sources (ponds, wells or

      
      

      
      
cisterns) are not regulated by

      
      

      
      
plumbing codes that ban the use

      
      

      
      
of lead pipes, and those

      
      

      
      
families may be drinking

      
      

      
      
potentially hazardous water.

      
      

      
      


      
      

      
      
Water from private sources is

      
      

      
      
soft and acidic which makes it

      
      

      
      
more corrosive than harder

      
      

      
      
commercial water. This water,

      
      

      
      
when it runs through pipes that

      
      

      
      
contain lead, can carry the lead

      
      

      
      
into household taps, said Joseph

      
      

      
      
Taraba, Extension agricultural

      
      

      
      
engineer for the University of

      
      

      
      
Kentucky College of Agriculture.

      
      

      
      


      
      

      
      
"In 1986 Congress passed the

      
      

      
      
Safe Drinking Water Act

      
      

      
      
Amendments which banned the use

      
      

      
      
of lead solder, lead pipe and

      
      

      
      
molded brass metal faucets

      
      

      
      
(which contain lead) in

      
      

      
      
household plumbing," said

      
      

      
      
Taraba.

      
      

      
      


      
      

      
      
There is a health concern if

      
      

      
      
lead exceeds 50 parts per

      
      

      
      
billion which is the standard

      
      

      
      
set by the Environmental

      
      

      
      
Protection Agency, he said. This

      
      

      
      
standard is under review to

      
      

      
      
reduce it to 10 or 20 parts per

      
      

      
      
billion. Lead can affect young

      
      

      
      
children more than adults. A

      
      

      
      
child's mental and physical

      
      

      
      
development may be stunted

      
      

      
      
permanently by lead poisoning.

      
      

      
      
Infants, since they are even

      
      

      
      
smaller and depend on water for

      
      

      
      
their source of food, as in baby

      
      

      
      
formula, have a greater danger

      
      

      
      
potential.

      
      

      
      


      
      

      
      
"Private water source owners can

      
      

      
      
take steps to reduce lead in

      
      

      
      
their water consumption," Taraba

      
      

      
      
said. "Water should be run until

      
      

      
      
it is as cold as it will get

      
      

      
      
before drinking or cooking. This

      
      

      
      
will flush out any lead that may

      
      

      
      
have accumulated over a period

      
      

      
      
of time (if it stands longer

      
      

      
      
than six hours). This should be

      
      

      
      
done with each faucet in the

      
      

      
      
house, as running just one

      
      

      
      
faucet will not get rid of all

      
      

      
      
the lead."

      
      

      
      


      
      

      
      
Water flushed does not have to

      
      

      
      
be wasted. It can be saved and

      
      

      
      
used for washing dishes or

      
      

      
      
clothes, he said. Also,

      
      

      
      
homeowners should never cook

      
      

      
      
with water taken from the

      
      

      
      
hot-water tap. Use cold water

      
      

      
      
and heat it on the stove.

      
      

      
      


      
      

      
      
Calcite filters can control

      
      

      
      
water corrosiveness and limit

      
      

      
      
lead, Taraba said. They should

      
      

      
      
be installed in the water line

      
      

      
      
between any lead connections or

      
      

      
      
lead-soldered pipes.

      
      

      
      


      
      

      
      
"Carbon filters, sand filters

      
      

      
      
and cartridge filters do not

      
      

      
      
remove lead or prevent

      
      

      
      
corrosion," Taraba said. "Water

      
      

      
      
softeners may reduce some of the

      
      

      
      
lead coming into the home, but

      
      

      
      
they also can make the water

      
      

      
      
more corrosive and add to lead

      
      

      
      
poisoning potential."

      
      

      
      


      
      

      
      
Non-lead solders are now

      
      

      
      
available for copper pipes, he

      
      

      
      
said.

       VEGETABLE IMPORTS

      
      

      
      


      
      

      
      
Vegetable imports are on the

      
      

      
      
rise; they reached almost five

      
      

      
      
billion pounds last season and

      
      

      
      
are expected to rise more this

      
      

      
      
year.

      
      

      
      


      
      

      
      
"We are in fact a vegetable

      
      

      
      
trade deficit area," said

      
      

      
      
Forrest Stegelin, Extension

      
      

      
      
economist for the University of

      
      

      
      
Kentucky College of Agriculture.

      
      

      
      


      
      

      
      
The deficit continues to widen

      
      

      
      
in spite of an eight percent

      
      

      
      
increase in exports, Stegelin

      
      

      
      
said. Exports still fall behind

      
      

      
      
their peak in 1981.

      
      

      
      


      
      

      
      
The nationwide vegetable market

      
      

      
      
affects Kentucky vegetable

      
      

      
      
markets, as well, he said. The

      
      

      
      
1988 drought seriously affected

      
      

      
      
snap bean, sweet corn and green

      
      

      
      
pea production for processing.

      
      

      
      
In fact, the 1988 prospective

      
      

      
      
canning acreage fell short of

      
      

      
      
what was produced in 1987.

      
      

      
      


      
      

      
      
"Drought reduced supplies caused

      
      

      
      
the consumer price index for

      
      

      
      
process vegetables to rise six

      
      

      
      
percent, as well," Stegelin

      
      

      
      
said. "Vegetable supplies are

      
      

      
      
expected to rise until it

      
      

      
      
replaces those that were lost to

      
      

      
      
the drought."

      
      

      
      


      
      

      
      
Specialty vegetables are not

      
      

      
      
expected to increase in Kentucky

      
      

      
      
production, he said. Mushrooms

      
      

      
      
and specialty vegetables like

      
      

      
      
Chinese vegetables will see some

      
      

      
      
increase. However, Kentucky will

      
      

      
      
continue to produce the

      
      

      
      
"mainstay of what most people

      
      

      
      
prefer."

      
      

      
      


      
      

      
      
Although this has been a

      
      

      
      
health-conscious country

      
      

      
      
recently, per capita vegetable

      
      

      
      
production fell slightly to 325

      
      

      
      
pounds, Stegelin said. On the

      
      

      
      
other hand, the nine major

      
      

      
      
processing vegetables' value

      
      

      
      
increased one percent.

forecast, but 11% more than the

cutting. Generally favorable

      
      

      
      
curing weather produced good

      
      

      
      
color and houseburn was light.

weather for handling the

       SOYBEAN VARIETIES

      
      

      
      


      
      

      
      
Farmers need to find the soybean

      
      

      
      
variety that will provide

      
      

      
      
maximum profit and this takes

      
      

      
      
careful consideration, said a

      
      

      
      
University of Kentucky

      
      

      
      
agronomist.

      
      

      
      


      
      

      
      
Soybeans come in wide varieties

      
      

      
      
to suit a farmer's needs in all

      
      

      
      
areas like maturity, yielding

      
      

      
      
ability, disease resistance,

      
      

      
      
standability and shattering

      
      

      
      
resistance, said James Herbek,

      
      

      
      
Extension grain crops specialist

      
      

      
      
for the U.K. College of

      
      

      
      
Agriculture.

      
      

      
      


      
      

      
      
Soybean maturity is controlled

      
      

      
      
by day length and the area of

      
      

      
      
the state the farmer's crop is

      
      

      
      
in determines the length of time

      
      

      
      
before the variety flowers,

      
      

      
      
Herbek said. For example,

      
      

      
      
earlier maturing varieties need

      
      

      
      
only a few hours of nighttime to

      
      

      
      
flower, while a late variety

      
      

      
      
needs longer hours. Northern

      
      

      
      
Kentucky is best for earlier

      
      

      
      
varieties and the later

      
      

      
      
varieties mature better in

      
      

      
      
southern Kentucky.

      
      

      
      


      
      

      
      
"When Kentucky has unusual

      
      

      
      
weather or rainfall, an early

      
      

      
      
variety may out yield the

      
      

      
      
farmer's regular variety," he

      
      

      
      
said.

      
      

      
      


      
      

      
      
Farmers also should consider

      
      

      
      
results from Kentucky soybean

      
      

      
      
performance tests that tell

      
      

      
      
which varieties had the best

      
      

      
      
yield under various conditions,

      
      

      
      
Herbek said. These are available

      
      

      
      
at the county Extension agent's

      
      

      
      
office.

      
      

      
      


      
      

      
      
Soybeans should stand well and

      
      

      
      
resist lodging (falling over in

      
      

      
      
the field) and shattering, or

      
      

      
      
the ability to keep seeds in the

      
      

      
      
pod, he said.

      
      

      
      


      
      

      
      
"Disease resistance in varieties

      
      

      
      
is not only wise, but it's

      
      

      
      
economical as well," said

      
      

      
      
Herbek. "Soybean cyst nematode

      
      

      
      
is a big problem in Kentucky and

      
      

      
      
finding resistant varieties will

      
      

      
      
reduce costs for nematicides."

      
      

      
      


      
      

      
      
There are nematode-resistant

      
      

      
      
varieties for all maturity

      
      

      
      
groups in Kentucky, he added.
