   Items of human interest... 


 

   
 
(Davenport)
 

  Iowans overlooked by the 

national recovery are being 

retrained for the occupation of 

unemployment. 

   Out of work Iowans gathered 

in the Quad Cities union hall 

this week for training in the 

skills they never acquired as 

fully employed workers. Skills 

like balancing a budget when 

income is cut by 70 percent; 

fiing free things to do with 

free time; and coping with the 

pressures of joblessness. 

   Program organizer Sylvia 

Banes says the classes have 

nothing to do with finding a 

job. Rather they teach 

surviving until the students 

find a job. 

   Ken Welzenback, a Davenport 

city councilman whose work as 

an electrician disappeared, 

said the classes teach 

something as simple as 

planning a day. He said when 

work disappears it can be 

devastating to people who are 

used to scheduling their lives 

around work. 

   The program is funded by an 

Eastern Iowa Community College 

Association grant. 

   Organizers say they 

acknowledge that unemployment 

is a semi-permanent condition 

for as many as 15 percent of 

all Quad City residents. 

   The students, all from 

different former walks of life, 

share cigarettes, meals and 

ideas in the seminars which 

feature sessions by different 

unemployment experts. 

   One session teaches soup 

making from shreds of 

inexpensive meat. Then the 

soup is used to feed the 

unemployed students at about 42 

cents per serving. 

   Another session encourages 

the out-of-work students to 

exchange the feelings and 

frustrations brought on by 

their unemployment.
