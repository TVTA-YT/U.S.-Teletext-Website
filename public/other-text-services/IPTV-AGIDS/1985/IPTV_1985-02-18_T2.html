   It was a two-dog night for 

Robbie Campbell. 

   Rescue workers found the boy 

Sunday... huddled in a thicket 

with two furry puppies to keep 

warm. He was lost for 12 hours 

in thick woods and sub-

freezing temperatures near 

Mount Airy, North Carolina. 

   Authorities said two-year-

old Robbie wandered away from 

his home shortly after noon 

Saturday... when one of his 

puppies chased a cat onto an 

abandoned homestead. The boy 

ran after him. 

   More than 400 volunteers 

searchor Robbie through the 

night... and two women found 

Robbie early yesterday morning. 

He was curled up with two of 

his dogs about a quarter mile 

from his house... wearing only 

lightweight clothes and NO hat 

or gloves. 

   The boy's grandfather 

credited the dogs with saving 

his life  saying  ''If he 

hadn't had the dogs with him... 

it could have turned into a 

tragedy.'' 

   Robbie is two weeks shy of 

his third birthday. 

   

 

   Under a 50-year-old Kentucky 

law, a job is NOT a thing of 

value. Therefore, it IS legal 

to promise a job in return for 

a vote. 

   Or so a Kentucky judge has 

ruled. 

   Clinton Circuit Judge Philip 

Morgan based his ruling 

Saturday on a 1934 decision by 

the Kentucky Court of Appeals. 

   Although state law forbids a 

candidate to offer money or 

other things of value in 

return for votes... the 1934 

ruling said promises of 

employment in such cases are 

not illegal. 

   The ruling has never been 

overturned. 

   The current case stems from 

allegations by an unsuccessful 

school board candidate in 

south-central Kentucky last 

November. He charged that his 

opponent won the election by 

promising people jobs if they 

would vote for him.
