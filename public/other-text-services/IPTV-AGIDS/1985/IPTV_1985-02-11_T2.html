 Iowa Sports At a Glance= 

 

   Basketball players from 

Marycrest and Morningside 

colleges won the N-A-I-A 

District 15 mens and womens 

player of the week honors. 

Robbie Lehr, a 6-foot-1 senior 

center for Morningside won the 

women's honor for scoring 67 

points in three games last week.

 The men's winner was 

rycrest's Tony Watkins, a 6-

foot-2 senior guard from 

Chicago. He led league-leading 

Marycrest to three victories 

last week with a total of 80 

points. 

 

 

   Michigan moved to the top of 

the Big Ten standings during 

the weekend but the road ahead 

for a Wolverine conference 

championship won't be easy. 

Michigan improved its mark to 9-

and-2 with a 57-to-45 win over 

Illinois. Iowa was idle on 

Saturday and fell one half game 

behind Michigan. But the 

Hawkeyes have an easier 

schedule with more home games 

than the Wolverines...including 

next week's showdown in Iowa 

City with Michigan. 

 The Big Ten race is dwindling 

to a two-team affair with 

Illinois' defeat. Five teams 

are tied for third at 6-and-5, 

including Illinois. 

 

 

   Iowa State's 6th-ranked 

wrestling team won 9 of 10 

matches this afternoon in 

Minneapolis to gain a 39-to-2 

victory over Minnesota. The 

Cyclones were led by all-

Americans John Thorn and Joe 

Gibbons... who recorded pins 

at 126 and 142 pounds 

respectively. I-S-U improves to 

18-and-6 on the season, while 

Minnesota falls to 9-and-9. The 

Cyclones need just one more 

dual victory to tie the all-

time Iowa State mark of 19 

wins set in 1976. The Cyclones 

are at home against Northern 

Iowa next Friday. 

 

 

   Meanwhile, the only 

wrestling team to beat first-

ranked Iowa during the last 

two years...Oklahoma State...

will wrestle the Hawkeyes next 

Saturday at 7:30 p-m in the 

Carver-Hawkeye Arena. Iowa is 

now 16-0 on the season. 

 

   In college basketball, 

Malcolm Thomas credits his 

defense action against Iowa 

State's Barry Stevens as the 

key to Missouri's 73-to-70 

victory over Iowa State in Ames 

yesterday. Thomas scored 22 

points...5 above his average...

.but he said his tenacious 

defense was more important to 

the Tigers' fourth straight win.

 That defense held Cyclone 

scoring leader Barry Stevens to 

only 14 points on 5 out of 20 

shooting from the field. 

 

 

   Northern Iowa's Randy 

Kraayenbrink is now in third 

place on U-N-I's all-time 

basketball career scoring list. 

Kraayenbrink led the Panthers 

with 29 points against Eastern 

Illinois last night. The junior 

guard now has 1-Thousand-295 

points...leaving only Ron 

Lemons and career scoring 

leader Bill McCoy ahead of him. 

McCoy had 1-Thousand-713 points.

 Despite Kraayenbrink's 

efforts, Eastern Illinois still 

defeated Northern Iowa 90-to-

82 yesterday.

(Golf)
 

 

   Mark O'Meara yesterday 

became the first player since 

1983 to win back-to-back P-G-A 

Tournaments. O'Meara held off 

Craig Stadler to win the 500-

thousand-dollar Hawaiian Open 

by a single stroke. 

   Last week, O'Meara took home 

the winner's purse in the Bing 

Crosby National Pro-Am. His 

earnings for the young season 

now total almost 184-thousand 

dollars. That's good enough to 

vault past Lanny Wadkins to 

the Number One spot on the 

money list. 

   Wadkins  who skipped the 

Honolulu event  began the year 

by winning the Bob Hope Desert 

Classic. Two weeks later, he 

captured the Los Angeles Open. 

In between, Calvin Peete won 

the Phoenix Open. 

   So, with the season five 

tournaments old, just three 

players are champions. The 

rest of the golfers must wait 

until this week's San Diego 

Open to try their luck again.

 Orr of United Press 

International in Chicago: 

 

   NO one wants to be mayor of 

Stanley, Louisiana. 

   And nobody wants to run for 

any other office in the town. 

   Stanley has five political 

offices that are supposed to be 

filled in an election on March 

30th. Nobody has filed as a 

candidate. 

   Johnny Liles has been mayor 

for eight years. He said  

''Louisiana politics has left 

a bad taste in our mouths.'' 

   It costs 37 dollars 50 cents 

to register as a candidate. In 

addition, town officials don't 

get paid. Liles said  ''It's a 

constant grind, a real 

headache.'' 

   Liles said the town may 

become unincorporated or become 

annexed to another town. 

 

   West Germany plans to dig a 

hole  and dig and dig. 

   The hole will be up to nine 

miles deep, making it the 

deepest in the world. 

   The German government 

announced yesterday the hole 

will be bored in the Black 

Forest or in Bavaria near the 

Czech border. 

   It will cost up to 138-

Million dollars. 

   Germany's research and 

technology ministry says the 

hole will provide information 

about the Earth's crust.

   The spotted newt is 

important to New Hampshire's 

tourism business. 

   New Hampshire's high school 

biology students know that and 

went to the state legislature 

to say so. 

   Jennifer Sula explained that 

bigger animals like raccoons 

eat newts, so it's a link in 

the food chain. Sula said  

''Any disruption of the 

natural order may reduce the 

state's attractiveness to 

tourists.'' 

   Michael Mullen said the newt 

is important in checking acid 

rain. He said most of its eggs 

do not hatch in a body of rain 

with a high acidity level. 

   The stu■nts were testifying 

about a bill to make the 

spotted newt New Hampshire's 

official state amphibian. 

   

 

   A congressman and at least 

16-hundred other people want 

the Rye, New York, post office 

to keep its dog. 

   A regional supervisor said 

Thursday the mascot  named 

Zipcode  could not stay at the 

post office because it violated 

health and safety rules. 

   Representative Joseph 

DioGuardi sent a letter to U-S 

Postmaster General Paul Carlin 

yesterday and asked him to 

overturn the decision. 

   He said 16-hundred people 

had signed a petition asking 

that Zipcode stay on as a 

mascot. 

   The workers at the Rye post 

office have had Zipcode for 

nine years. Her owner 

abandoned her and the post 

office adopted her. 

   A spokesman for the Postal 

Service says the matter will be 

reviewed.

   ''How do you select good 

wine for a Frenchman?'' That 

was a question asked by 

American astronaut Rhea Seddon 

when she and the other crew 

members on next month's shuttle 

flight had a dinner welcoming 

French astronaut Patrick Baudry 

 
(BOH'-dree)
 

 to their mission last fall. 

Seddon says they finally 

decided to serve Texas wine 

from a Texas vineyard. And 

it's good they didn't try the 

French wine route... since 

Baudry has a cellar holding 

four-thousand bottles of wine. 

Baudry says wine is both art 

and ''a human part of people''..

. and it's unfortunate it 

includes alcohol. The 

connoisseur says wine is ''not 

my first occupation... but 

when I have time I like very 

much to go to visit some 

vineyards.'' 

 

   A Soviet film studio's 

production of ''Bambi'' has 

been thrown off schedule 

because three of the four deer 

in the movie ended up on 

someone's dinner table. The 

deer vanished from the set in 

the Crimea 
 
(Krigh-MEE'-uh)
 

, where the movie  based on 

Felix Salten's children's book 

 is being filmed. A local 

newspaper 
 
(the daily Selyskaya 

Shisny)
 

 reported that police 

discovered the deer had been 

stolen by three people they 

described as ''notorious 

freeloaders''... who allegedly 

slaughtered them for a 

birthday feast. And according 

to a German news agency, the 

deer-slayers were sentenced to 

four to six years in prison... 

while the Yalta film studio 

was awarded about six-thousand 

dollars in damages for the 

loss of its stars.
